The best website hosting and maintenance services pair reliable hosting with updates, backups, security monitoring, and content edits. For WordPress, SiteCare, WP Buffs, and GoWP are specialist options. Codebru covers custom server administration.

Hosting alone keeps a site online, but it does not keep it secure, current, or fast. This article sorts four providers by service model, so you can see which ones cover WordPress care, which handle custom stacks, and where cost differences come from. It also covers when a build-and-maintain partner makes sense and what skipping maintenance can cost you.

## What Are Website Hosting and Maintenance Services?

Website maintenance services are recurring tasks that keep a site secure, updated, and working after launch. Hosting supplies the server; maintenance supplies the people who look after what runs on it.

Most plans bundle some mix of the following:

- Core, plugin, and theme updates
- Scheduled backups
- Security monitoring and malware cleanup
- Speed and uptime checks
- Content edits, often capped by plan tier

The market behind these services is large. Dataintelo reports that website maintenance services reached a global valuation of $14.8 billion in 2025. Within that market, security management made up 31.4% of spending by service type, which shows where site owners put their money first.

## 1. SiteCare: Premium WordPress Site Health

SiteCare is the premium option for WordPress sites that have outgrown freelancer support. It targets midmarket businesses and sells a complete site health package rather than a bare hosting account.

The package centers on CoreSpeed managed hosting, SOC 2 compliance, and guaranteed Core Web Vitals performance. For a business that needs documented security controls or has to meet speed targets, those commitments matter more than a long feature list. Updates are tested on a staging copy before they go live, which helps keep a patch from breaking the site.

That staging step matters for a practical reason. In 2025, 91% of vulnerabilities found in the WordPress ecosystem were in plugins rather than core software, according to grigora.co's vendor-reported figure. Plugins need frequent patching, and each patch can break something, so testing before release lowers the risk.

SiteCare sits at a premium price tier. That fits teams where downtime or slow pages carry real revenue costs, and it is likely more than a small brochure site needs.

Best for: midmarket WordPress sites that need managed hosting, compliance, and performance guarantees in one plan.

Before you pick SiteCare, compare its guarantees against what your site really needs. A site that earns revenue through its pages gains the most from documented speed targets and tested updates. A small brochure site that changes twice a year may pay for protections it never uses.

## 2. WP Buffs: 24/7 WordPress Management

WP Buffs is a budget-tier option built around round-the-clock WordPress management. Its plans cover weekly updates, security monitoring, and speed optimization, and higher tiers add unlimited content edits.

Protection is part of the base offer. Plans include malware removal and four daily offsite backups, so a site can be restored from a recent copy if something goes wrong. That backup frequency is useful for sites that change often, such as stores or news pages.

WP Buffs also offers a white-label service for agencies that want to resell WordPress support under their own name. If you run a web design shop, that can add a care plan to your menu without hiring a support team.

The main limit is scope. The model centers on WordPress management, so you should confirm separately where your site will be hosted. Because the card does not state that hosting is bundled, treat it as a care service first.

Best for: small and midsize WordPress sites, and agencies that want a budget-friendly support partner.

When you compare WP Buffs with a premium provider, look at what each tier adds. Higher tiers raise the edit allowance, so a site that changes weekly will outgrow a basic tier faster than one that is mostly static. Ask how quickly the team responds to urgent issues and how a restore request is handled.

## 3. GoWP: Hybrid AI and Human Edits

GoWP combines an AI editor with human help, and the mix changes by plan tier. The AI editor lets you make self-service changes to your site. Higher tiers add human edit tasks, so a person handles the changes that an AI tool should not touch alone.

This hybrid model suits owners who make small updates often, such as swapping a headline or fixing a page layout. Rather than waiting on a ticket, you can try the change yourself first and hand off the harder work.

Entry is low-risk. GoWP offers a free tier with five AI edits, though that tier does not include backups. If you start there, set up your own backups before relying on it for anything important.

Support on every plan comes through a 24/7 help desk by email. GoWP sits in the budget tier, so its support is built around email and task queues, not the dedicated engineering team a premium provider like SiteCare offers. The cards give no response-time figures, so ask any provider for written support terms before you sign.

Best for: owners who want low-cost WordPress upkeep and are comfortable making simple edits themselves.

The trade-off with a hybrid model is control. An AI editor is quick for small changes, but you should review each edit before it goes live, especially on pages that drive sales or sign-ups. Keep human edit tasks for anything that touches layout across many pages.

## 4. Codebru: Custom Server Administration and Managed Hosting

Codebru suits sites that run on custom stacks rather than WordPress. It provides custom software development, LAMP and LEMP server administration, and managed hosting through Linode. Ongoing maintenance includes security hardening and performance tuning.

Its strength is server-level depth. Codebru handles database administration and Node balancer configuration, which matter once a site needs to spread traffic across several servers. Clients can bring their own Linode server or use a fully managed setup, so you keep a choice over who owns the infrastructure.

A caution on evidence: this profile rests on a single source, so confirm the details directly with Codebru before choosing it. Its pricing is not publicly listed, so you will need to ask for a quote.

Best for: businesses running custom applications on LAMP or LEMP stacks that need hands-on server administration.

Custom stacks carry a different kind of risk than WordPress. There is no shared plugin ecosystem to patch, so the work centers on the operating system, the web server, the database, and the application code. When you talk to Codebru, ask who is on call when a server fails and how backups of the database are tested.

## What Happens When You Skip Website Maintenance?

Skipping maintenance saves a small monthly fee, but it can expose you to a much larger bill when something breaks. Set the two side by side.

On the cost side, maintenance plans are a modest recurring expense compared with the cost of recovery. On the risk side, themarketingagency.ca documents one WordPress hack that cost $15,000 to remediate. That is a single vendor-reported case, not an average, but it shows how recovery can be priced.

The exposure is concentrated in plugins. In 2025, 91% of WordPress ecosystem vulnerabilities were found in plugins rather than core software, per grigora.co's vendor-reported figure. A site with unpatched plugins carries most of its risk in the part that needs regular attention.

Use this short checklist when choosing a plan:

- Confirm whether hosting is included or sold separately
- Ask how often backups run and where they are stored
- Check whether updates are tested before release
- Find out how many content edits each tier allows
- Request written support terms, since response times vary by provider
- Match the plan to your platform: WordPress care, custom server work, or a build-and-maintain partner

For Webflow or Shopify sites, the platform handles servers, so plan for content, apps, and integrations instead.

## FAQs

### How much should you charge for website maintenance?

If you sell maintenance, price it against the scope you deliver, not a flat figure. Charge more when you include hosting, 24/7 monitoring, or unlimited edits, and less for a basic update-and-backup package.

### Is hosting included in maintenance plans?

Sometimes. SiteCare bundles CoreSpeed managed hosting with its care plan, and Codebru offers managed hosting through Linode. Other plans, such as WP Buffs and GoWP, are described as management and edit services without bundled hosting. Always confirm in writing what the plan covers before comparing prices.

### What are website maintenance services?

Website maintenance services are recurring tasks that keep a site secure, updated, and working after launch. They typically include core, plugin, and theme updates, scheduled backups, security monitoring, speed checks, and content edits. Some providers add hosting. The market was valued at $14.8 billion in 2025.

### What are the top 5 website builders?

This article compares maintenance providers, not site builders, and the research behind it does not rank builders. Pick a builder based on your platform needs, then choose a maintenance plan that supports it. WordPress, Webflow, and Shopify are common platforms, and each has different upkeep needs.

### What is hosting in a website?

Hosting is the server space that stores your website's files and delivers them to visitors. Without it, your site is not reachable online. Hosting keeps a site available, but it does not by itself handle updates, security monitoring, or content changes, which is what maintenance services add.

### How do I maintain a Webflow or Shopify site?

On Webflow and Shopify, the platform runs the servers and core software, so your work shifts to content, apps, and integrations. Review installed apps regularly, keep backups of your content and data, and check pages for speed and broken links. The research here does not cover these platforms in detail, so follow each platform's own guidance.

## Conclusion

The right choice depends on your platform. WordPress sites are well served by a specialist care plan from SiteCare, WP Buffs, or GoWP, custom server stacks suit Codebru, and a custom build with upkeep from one team suits a partner like Scubafy Solutions. Hosting alone does not cover security or updates, so any plan should be judged on what maintenance it includes.

Start by listing what your site needs: your platform, how often content changes, and whether hosting must be bundled. Then ask each shortlisted provider for written terms on backups, update testing, and support response times before you commit.
